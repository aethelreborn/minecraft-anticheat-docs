# Appendix D: Historical Evolution of Minecraft Anticheats

Early public servers relied on simple plugins that checked maximum speed and basic flight. As cheat clients proliferated, the community produced NoCheat and later NoCheatPlus, which introduced more structured checks and configuration. The 1.8 era saw heavy focus on combat because of the prevalence of that version in PvP. The 1.9 combat changes and subsequent movement additions forced major redesigns. The rise of predictive engines in the late 2010s and early 2020s marked a qualitative improvement in movement detection accuracy. Parallel commercial development produced polished, frequently updated products with extensive combat heuristic suites. The current landscape is a mixture of strong open-source prediction cores, commercial all-in-one solutions, and specialized combat overlays, with increasing interest in statistical and machine-learning methods for the hardest ghost-client cases.
